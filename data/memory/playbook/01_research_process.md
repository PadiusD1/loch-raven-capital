# Research process and the earnings cycle

Firm playbook chapter 1. Distilled in our own words from the author's Wall Street Prep Sell Side Equity Research Certification notes. Paraphrased guidance, not course text.

## What the research desk is for

A research desk exists to produce investment ideas, the intelligence behind them, and access to the people who know the business. The rating is the smallest part of that value. Clients pay far more for industry work, company work and management access than for a buy or sell label, so a verdict without a reasoned thesis is worth very little.

## Coverage discipline

An analyst covers a focused universe, usually ten to thirty names in one sector, because depth beats breadth. A name earns coverage when the desk has a genuinely different view, when it sits next to names already covered, when other desks overlook it, or when it is simply too important to ignore. Sectors follow the eleven GICS sectors, and macro is researched alongside them rather than as a sector.

## The earnings cycle has three phases

Before the print, tell clients what to watch, restate the thesis and check estimates. On the day, compare actual results with the model and with the thesis, find what matters that is not obvious, and above all compare the new guidance with your own expectations. After the print, summarize, reconcile the results with the thesis, and update estimates so the view stays differentiated from consensus.

## Earnings day mechanics

Results arrive in a press release just before the open or just after the close, with the three statements attached. The call follows within the hour. The first analyst questions are the most revealing, because they go straight at the issue everyone is worried about.

## Differentiate or do not publish

Anyone can repeat the obvious conclusion. The job is a view that differs from consensus and is backed by evidence. When the desk agrees with consensus, say so plainly and rate the name accordingly, because a HOLD with a clear reason is more useful than a forced opinion.
