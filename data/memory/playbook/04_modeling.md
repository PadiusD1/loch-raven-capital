# Three statement modeling

Firm playbook chapter 4. Distilled in our own words from the author's Wall Street Prep Sell Side Equity Research Certification notes. Paraphrased guidance, not course text.

## How the statements link

Net income flows into retained earnings on the balance sheet and to the top of the cash flow statement. Every line should be driven by an assumption or a schedule so the model moves as one system when an input changes.

## Build order

Start with historical results and assumptions, project the income statement down to operating profit, build the supporting schedules for working capital, fixed assets and depreciation, debt and interest, and equity, then finish the income statement, build the cash flow statement and carry ending cash to the balance sheet.

## Revenue from the bottom up

Build revenue from price and volume or from segments rather than one growth rate. Working capital schedules driven by days receivable, days inventory and days payable produce the change in working capital that feeds operating cash flow.

## The balance check

The balance sheet must balance. If assets do not equal liabilities plus equity, a link is broken, and no conclusion from the model can be trusted until it is fixed. A revolver or cash sweep keeps cash from going negative, and the interest to cash circularity is handled with an iteration switch.

## Conventions

Hard coded inputs are blue, formulas are black and links to other sheets are green, so a reviewer can see at a glance which numbers are assumptions.
