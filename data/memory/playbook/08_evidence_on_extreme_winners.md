# What the evidence says about extreme winners

Firm playbook chapter 8. Our summary of Anna Yartseva, The Alchemy of Multibagger Stocks, Birmingham City University Centre for Applied Finance and Economics working paper 33, February 2025 (CC BY NC SA), with our own caveats.

## Findings worth testing

Among stocks that later rose tenfold, free cash flow yield was the strongest recurring trait, profitability levels mattered more than growth rates, and asset growth running ahead of EBITDA growth was a warning sign. Price to earnings was a weak variable compared with free cash flow to price and book to market. Rising policy rates weighed on the expected returns of growth stocks.

## Why these are hypotheses, not rules

The study only looks at companies that already became big winners, so it has no control group and cannot say whether these traits pick future winners out of the whole market. Its sample is selected on the outcome, which builds in look ahead bias. Its finding that buying near a twelve month low worked is the riskiest to copy, because inside a sample of eventual winners every dip was a buying chance by construction, while in the full market the same rule buys falling knives.

## How the firm uses it

Treat these traits as ideas for the quant screen and as context in committee debate. A trait only earns weight in real decisions after it passes the firm's own point in time test on its own graded history.
