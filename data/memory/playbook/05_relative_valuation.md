# Relative valuation with multiples

Firm playbook chapter 5. Distilled in our own words from the author's Wall Street Prep Sell Side Equity Research Certification notes. Paraphrased guidance, not course text.

## What comps can and cannot say

A multiple above the right peer group may indicate overvaluation and one below may indicate undervaluation. It never proves either, because the peers can all be mispriced together.

## Match the numerator to the denominator

Equity multiples such as price to earnings, price to book, price to sales and PEG put equity value over an equity measure. Enterprise multiples such as EV to EBITDA, EV to sales, EV to EBIT and EV to free cash flow put enterprise value, market cap plus net debt, over a measure that belongs to all capital providers.

## Choosing the multiple

Price to earnings is the most common but needs positive earnings, ignores debt and cash flow, and misleads for cyclicals whose peak earnings make the stock look cheap at the top. EV to EBITDA is neutral to capital structure and compares operating performance across different leverage, though it ignores capital intensity. Price to book suits banks and fails for companies whose value is intangible. Price to sales and EV to sales suit early stage and loss making companies. EV to free cash flow is less distorted by non cash charges.

## What moves multiples

Faster growth earns a higher multiple. Rising rates and inflation compress multiples, most of all for long duration growth companies. Steadier and more predictable earnings earn a rerating. Defensive sectors hold their multiples better in recessions.

## Adjustments

Treat operating leases as debt when comparing companies with different lease use. Buybacks raise earnings per share and lower the price to earnings multiple with nothing else changing. Markets price the future, so prefer forward multiples, and only reach several years out for fast growers with little current profit.
