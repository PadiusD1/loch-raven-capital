# Financial statement analysis

Firm playbook chapter 2. Distilled in our own words from the author's Wall Street Prep Sell Side Equity Research Certification notes. Paraphrased guidance, not course text.

## Read all three statements together

The income statement shows profitability over a period on an accrual basis. The balance sheet shows position at a point in time and must satisfy assets equal liabilities plus equity. The cash flow statement reconciles net income to cash through operating, investing and financing activity. A thesis built on one statement alone misses how the three connect.

## Operating working capital

Measure operating working capital as current assets minus current liabilities while leaving out cash and short term debt. Cash is not an operating asset and short term debt is financing, so excluding both isolates the working capital the business actually runs on.

## Model drivers, not percentages

Break revenue into price times volume and cost of goods into unit cost times units. A blind growth percentage hides whether a company is winning on pricing, on volume or on neither.

## The margin ladder

Gross margin reflects pricing power and input costs. EBITDA margin shows core cash operating profit before depreciation. Operating margin adds depreciation and operating expense control. Net margin comes after interest and tax. When a margin moves, walk down the ladder to find the rung that moved.

## Returns and the DuPont split

Return on equity is levered and can be flattered by debt. Return on invested capital, operating profit after tax over invested capital, is the cleanest comparison of business quality across companies. The DuPont identity splits return on equity into net margin times asset turnover times the equity multiplier, which shows whether profitability, efficiency or leverage did the work.

## Liquidity, leverage and efficiency

Current and quick ratios measure short term strength. Debt to equity and interest coverage, operating profit over interest, measure balance sheet risk. Days sales outstanding, days inventory outstanding and days payables outstanding combine into the net operating cycle, and a lengthening cycle ties up cash.

## Free cash flow

Free cash flow is operating cash flow minus capital expenditure. Negative free cash flow is often heavy growth investment rather than weakness, so read the capex line before judging. Profit that never turns into cash is a warning sign, especially when a large gain sits below the operating line.

## Ratios are comparative

A ratio means little on its own. Compare it with peers and with the company's own history, and never build a thesis on one backward looking number.
