# Research reports and the stock pitch

Firm playbook chapter 7. Distilled in our own words from the author's Wall Street Prep Sell Side Equity Research Certification notes. Paraphrased guidance, not course text.

## What a report contains

A research report carries the thesis, the rating and price target, the model and valuation, the financials, industry and competitor context, a risks section, and disclosures at the back. The rating and target sit at the top so a busy reader gets the answer first.

## Report types

Initiations start coverage and are long. Earnings previews and reviews bracket each quarterly print. Deep dives explore a theme. Flash notes are one or two pages reacting to news. A covered name hears from the analyst roughly every quarter and whenever news breaks.

## The first page of an earnings review

Lead with a short summary paragraph and about three bullets that say what changed and what it means for the thesis. Valuation rests on a DCF and on comparable multiples. Chart patterns are not a valuation method.

## The two minute pitch

A stock pitch is a tight two minute case built on the thesis and its pillars. Skip the generic company description and the facts everyone already knows. Cut to the one or two elements that make the view different, then say what would prove it wrong.

## Communicating with clients

Match the language to the client. Present a contrarian view clearly and logically with the evidence behind it, and treat critical feedback as information about the thesis rather than an attack on it.
