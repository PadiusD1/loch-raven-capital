# Thesis and due diligence

Firm playbook chapter 3. Distilled in our own words from the author's Wall Street Prep Sell Side Equity Research Certification notes. Paraphrased guidance, not course text.

## A thesis is a variant view

A real investment thesis disagrees with consensus about future results or valuation. It looks forward and does not recap the past. Keep it to two to four supporting pillars, because a laundry list signals that no single reason is strong enough.

## Where upside comes from

A strong buy does not need the multiple to expand. It can rest entirely on results that beat consensus, so the return comes from earnings growth rather than a rerating. Value drivers are higher volume, higher prices, lower cost of sales and buybacks. A higher multiple is justified by faster growth, steadier and more predictable revenue, or share gains, never by rising interest rates, which compress multiples.

## Due diligence scope

Check historical growth and profitability, the debt and equity structure, governance, the supply chain, research and development, capital spending, intellectual property and the debt maturity schedule. Channel checks with suppliers, customers and distributors verify demand from outside the company. The annual report supplies the business description, statements, management discussion and risk factors, while consensus estimates come from the Street.

## Moats

Durable advantages come from intangible assets, network effects, switching costs, cost advantages and efficient scale. A government granted monopoly is a different thing and should be analyzed as regulatory risk.

## Catalysts

A catalyst is a future event that can change the story, such as an earnings print, a pending deal or a scheduled data release. A track record of past beats is not a catalyst. Every thesis should name its catalyst and its date.

## When to change the rating

Downgrade on a material fundamental change such as a guidance cut, not on noise or on events the market already expected. Rating scales belong to each firm. A price that runs past the target is normal and is a prompt to revisit, not proof the thesis broke.

## Macro sensitivity

Cyclical sectors such as energy, financials and industrials move most with the economy, and defensive sectors such as staples, health care and utilities move least. Within a company, operating leverage from fixed costs and financial leverage from debt amplify the cycle. Short cycles are driven by monetary policy, and leading signals include the yield curve, capacity utilization and industrial production.
